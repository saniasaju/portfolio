Overview

Imagine you’re planning your first camping trip.

You know you need a tent, but then the questions begin. What type should you buy? How much should you spend? Which features actually matter? And is the cheaper option still durable enough?

Now imagine answering all of that while moving through crowded menus, long product lists, technical specifications, and complicated filters.

That is the experience CampInn was designed to simplify. Users can browse gear, filter products, review product information, add items to their cart, and complete purchases through a focused mobile flow.

Challenge

Beginner and budget-conscious campers often encounter too many options, confusing filters, and lengthy checkout processes, making it harder to choose the right gear with confidence.

Solution

I designed CampInn to make shopping simpler through clearer navigation, useful filtering, easier-to-scan product information, accessible support, and a more predictable checkout experience.

User Persona

To keep the project grounded in a real shopping context, I developed Chiaki: a part-time cook who enjoys camping and hiking but has limited time to compare products across multiple websites.

She wants affordable and durable gear, yet finds current shopping experiences complicated and time-consuming.

Her needs gave the project a clear focus: find suitable gear quickly, understand the options, stay within budget, and purchase with confidence.

User Journey Map

I mapped Chiaki’s journey from realising she needs a tent to completing the purchase, highlighting the moments where she feels overwhelmed, compares options, and needs clearer guidance.

This journey map helped me spot friction points in Chiaki’s shopping process and turn them into design opportunities, such as easier filtering and a smoother checkout experience.

Competitive Analysis

I reviewed outdoor and e-commerce experiences, including REI, Snow Peak, Amazon, Backcountry, and Cascade Designs. I compared their navigation, shopping experiences, and user needs. This revealed three opportunities for CampInn:

Combine strong filtering with simpler navigation

Make accessibility tools easy to find and use

Present product details clearly without overwhelming users

Information Architecture

I organised CampInn around four primary sections:

Home

Introduces relevant products, trusted brands, and quick entry points so users can begin exploring without feeling overwhelmed.

Shop

Organises products by need and helps users narrow their options through categories, listings, filters, and product details.

Cart

Lets users review products, quantities, prices, discounts, and purchase details before checkout.

Account

Keeps preferences, order activity, payment details, support, and settings accessible without crowding the main shopping experience.

Hi-Fi Design

I began with quick paper sketches to explore layouts and translate the user flow into screens. The strongest ideas moved into digital wireframes and a low-fidelity prototype, where I focused on keeping the experience simple, scannable, and task-driven. After testing and refining the structure, I developed the high-fidelity design using an outdoor-inspired visual system, clear product cards, familiar mobile shopping patterns, and a focused information hierarchy. The final prototype supports the complete purchase journey from product discovery to order confirmation.

Usability Testing

I tested the primary shopping flow with 5 participants in remote, unmoderated usability sessions lasting approximately 20–30 minutes. The objective was simple: identify where users became uncertain while navigating, discovering products, using accessibility support, and checking out. Testing revealed several areas that needed more attention:

Product categories needed to be clearer

Product cards needed to be easier to scan

Checkout needed to feel more predictable

Filtering needed more visibility

Product details needed stronger decision support

Order confirmation needed to provide closure

Key Iterations

Filter Visibility

I replaced Refine with the more familiar Filter, added a funnel icon, and moved the control into a more prominent position above the product list.

Accessibility Support

I increased the visibility of Text-to-Speech and Translate so non-native English speakers and users reviewing technical product details could access help without searching through menus.

Checkout Clarity

I simplified the form experience to reduce unnecessary input friction and make the path toward order completion easier to follow.

Accessibility

CampInn integrates accessibility into the shopping flow through Text-to-Speech and in-app Translate, positioned close to technical product information. Clear product details, intuitive navigation, and touch-friendly controls simplify browsing and purchasing.

Reflection

CampInn reinforced that clarity in UX is not only visual. It involves structure, language, hierarchy, and interaction. Every label and action can either increase or decrease user effort. The project encouraged me to think deliberately about how those decisions work together to build user confidence throughout the experience.

Next Steps

CampInn currently solves the core purchasing journey, but there are several opportunities to take it further. I would next explore:

  • Compare products side by side so users can choose gear more easily.
  • Improve accessibility and personalisation with better screen reader support, text size options, and more relevant recommendations.
  • Support beginners better through simple buying guides, community tips, and resources.

Those additions could help CampInn evolve from a place to buy camping equipment into a place where beginners can also learn how to choose it.
